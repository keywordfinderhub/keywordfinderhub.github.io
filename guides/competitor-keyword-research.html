Competitor Keyword Research: Learn from Sites That Already Rank

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Competitor keyword research is not copying. It is learning which keywords already bring traffic in your niche, which topics your competitors cover well, and which ones they missed. A competitor that ranks for a keyword has shown that the keyword is worth targeting and that a page on it can rank. Your job is to find the keywords where you can do better, not to copy their pages. This guide explains how to pick the right competitors, how to find their keywords, and how to turn the list into pages that are genuinely better.

Pick the right competitors to study

The best competitors to study are sites similar to yours in size and topic, not the biggest sites in the world. A small site that ranks well for many keywords in your niche is a better model than a huge site you cannot compete with yet. List three to five competitors that cover the same topics you do, and note what they do well. Avoid studying sites that are too different: a news site, a shop and a blog may all rank for a keyword, but they serve different intents and their keyword lists will mislead you.

Find the keywords they rank for

Use a keyword tool to enter a competitor's domain and see the keywords it ranks for, especially the ones that bring it the most traffic. Sort by traffic or by position, and look for keywords where the competitor ranks on page one with a page you could improve. Look for keywords the competitor ranks for on page two: those are often easier to win. Write down the keywords that fit your site, and ignore the ones that do not. A long list is not the goal; a short list of keywords you can win is.

Find the gaps they left

The most valuable competitor keywords are the gaps: topics a competitor covers thinly, questions it does not answer, or keywords it ranks for by accident with a page about something else. Open the competitor's page and ask what is missing: a step, an example, an updated fact, a clearer explanation, or a section for beginners. Those gaps are your opportunity. A page that fills a real gap is more useful than a page that repeats what the competitor already does well. Note the gaps next to each keyword in your list.

Turn the research into better pages

For each keyword you choose, write a page that is clearly better than the competitor's: more complete, more up to date, easier to read, or better organised. Do not copy their text or their structure; write your own page from your own knowledge. Add the details they missed, answer the questions they ignored, and use examples from your own experience. Publish the page, then track whether it climbs. Competitor research is a starting point, not a shortcut: the page still has to earn its ranking.

Quick tips

  • Study sites similar in size to yours
  • Look for page-two rankings you can win
  • Note the gaps, not just the keywords
  • Write a clearly better page, do not copy

Frequently asked questions

Is it okay to target a competitor's keywords?

Yes. Keywords are not owned by anyone. Targeting the same topic is normal, as long as you write your own original page and do not copy their content.

How many competitors should I study?

Three to five similar sites is enough to start. More than that creates a long list that is hard to act on.

What is a keyword gap?

A topic or question a competitor covers thinly or not at all, where you can write a better, more complete page and win the ranking.
