YouTube Keywords: Find Phrases People Search for on Video

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

YouTube is the second largest search engine in the world, and people search there differently from how they search on Google. They want to watch, not read: how to tie a tie, a tour of a place, a review of a product, a lesson in a skill. A video that targets the right keyword can be found for years, while a video with a vague title may never be found at all. This guide explains how YouTube search works, how to find video keywords, and how to use them in titles, descriptions and tags without misleading viewers.

How YouTube search is different

On YouTube, the searcher wants a video, so the keyword should match a topic that works well on video: a demonstration, a tutorial, a review, a tour or a story. A keyword that works as an article may not work as a video, and the reverse is true. YouTube also weighs watch time and engagement: a video that people watch to the end and interact with will rank better than a video with a good title that people abandon. The keyword gets the video found; the video itself has to keep the viewer watching. Both matter, and neither replaces the other.

Find video keywords with YouTube itself

The YouTube search box is the best free tool for video keywords. Type a topic and watch the suggestions; those are phrases people actually search for on YouTube. Look at the titles of popular videos in your niche, and note the words they repeat. Check the comments for questions viewers ask, because each question is a video idea. A keyword tool with a YouTube mode can add volume estimates, but the search box and the top videos are enough to start. Write down the exact phrases viewers use, because a small change in wording can mean a different audience.

Use the keyword in the title and description

Put the main keyword near the start of the video title, in a natural way, and repeat it once in the first lines of the description. Write a description that tells the viewer what the video covers and when, with timestamps for the main sections. Add a few relevant tags, but do not rely on tags alone, because titles and descriptions matter more. Use the keyword in the spoken content too, because YouTube can understand speech. A clear title that promises what the video delivers is better than a clever title that confuses the searcher.

Make a video that deserves to rank

A good keyword will not save a poor video. Start with a clear promise in the first seconds, show the result early, and keep the video focused on the topic the title promised. Cut out long introductions and dead time, and use chapters so viewers can jump to the part they need. Ask a question or invite a comment at a natural point, but do not beg for engagement. Track which videos get found by search and which do not, and make more of the kind that works. Keyword research for YouTube is about finding topics people want to watch, then making a video worth watching.

Quick tips

  • Use the YouTube search box for real phrases
  • Put the keyword near the start of the title
  • Add timestamps to the description
  • Keep viewers watching with a clear promise early

Frequently asked questions

What is a YouTube keyword?

A phrase people type into the YouTube search box to find a video. It should match a topic that works well on video, such as a tutorial, a review or a demonstration.

Do tags matter for YouTube keywords?

They help a little, but titles, descriptions and the spoken content matter more. Use a few relevant tags, and focus on a clear title and a good video.

How do I find video keyword ideas?

Use YouTube search suggestions, look at popular video titles in your niche, and read viewer comments for questions they ask.
