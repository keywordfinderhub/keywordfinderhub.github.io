Niche Keyword Ideas: Find Phrases in a Small Market

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

A niche is a narrow topic: not cooking, but gluten-free baking for beginners; not fitness, but strength training for people over sixty. Niche sites can rank faster because the competition is smaller and the audience is more specific. The challenge is finding enough keywords in a small topic without repeating yourself or targeting phrases nobody searches. This guide explains how to explore a niche for keywords, how to expand it without losing focus, and how to know when a niche is too small to support a site.

Map the niche before you search

Start by writing down the main topics inside your niche, the problems people in it face, the products they use, and the questions they ask. A niche has subtopics, and each subtopic has its own keywords. For gluten-free baking, the subtopics might be bread, cakes, substitutes, equipment and troubleshooting. For each subtopic, write down the questions and phrases people use. This map shows you where the keywords are and where the gaps are, before you open a keyword tool. A niche that looks small often has more subtopics than you first think.

Expand with variations, not with unrelated topics

To find more keywords in a niche, add variations to a core phrase: for beginners, without an ingredient, for a specific occasion, for a specific tool, or for a specific problem. Each variation is a new keyword that still belongs in the niche. Do not expand by adding unrelated topics, because that dilutes the site and confuses both readers and search engines. A niche site should go deep, not wide. Ten good pages on one subtopic are better than one page on ten unrelated topics.

Use the language of the niche

People in a niche use specific words: ingredient names, tool names, technique names and common problems. Learn those words from forums, groups, books and videos in the niche, and use them in your keyword list. A keyword that uses the right niche language will match the searcher's words and will rank better than a generic phrase. Avoid inventing terms that nobody uses, and avoid using a broad word when the niche has a precise one. The language of the niche is a signal that you belong in it.

Know when a niche is too small

A niche is too small when you cannot find at least fifty keywords with some search volume, or when the audience is so small that even ranking well brings few visitors. Before you commit, check a keyword tool for the main phrases in the niche and look at the volume. If the numbers are very low across the whole niche, consider a slightly broader topic, or a niche with a buying audience, such as a hobby where people buy equipment. A niche should be small enough to win, but large enough to matter.

Quick tips

  • Map subtopics before you open a tool
  • Expand with variations, not unrelated topics
  • Use the exact language of the niche
  • Check that the niche has at least fifty keywords

Frequently asked questions

What is a niche keyword?

A keyword in a narrow, specific topic, such as a particular hobby, diet, sport or profession. Niche keywords usually have less competition and a more specific audience.

How do I find keywords in a small niche?

Map the subtopics, add variations such as for beginners or without an ingredient, and learn the specific words people in the niche use in forums and groups.

Can a niche be too small?

Yes. If you cannot find at least fifty keywords with some search volume, the audience may be too small. Consider a slightly broader topic.
