Keyword Difficulty Explained: What It Means and How to Judge It

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Keyword difficulty is a score that tries to say how hard it will be to rank on the first page for a keyword. A low score suggests an easier win, a high score suggests a long fight. The score is useful, but it is only a guess made by a tool, and different tools give different numbers for the same keyword. This guide explains what goes into difficulty, how to look past the score at the real competition, and how to decide whether a keyword is worth your time. It will help you spend effort on keywords you can actually win.

What difficulty scores measure

Most difficulty tools look at the pages already ranking for a keyword: how strong those sites are, how many links point to their pages, and how well the pages match the keyword. A keyword where the top pages come from famous, well-linked sites will score as hard. A keyword where the top pages are thin, old or poorly matched will score as easy. The score is a summary, not a measurement of your chances. Your own site's strength, the quality of the page you can write, and how well you match the searcher's intent all matter, and no score knows those things about you.

Look at the real pages, not just the number

Before you trust a score, open the top ten results for the keyword and look at them. Are they strong, detailed pages from well-known sites, or are they short, outdated or off-topic? Can you write a page that is clearly better, more complete or more up to date? If the top results are weak, the keyword may be easier than the score suggests. If the top results are excellent and come from sites far stronger than yours, the keyword may be harder than the score suggests. This five-minute check is more useful than any single number.

Judge difficulty by your own site

A keyword that is hard for a new site may be easy for an established one, and the reverse is true. Consider your site's age, its existing traffic, and whether you already rank for related topics. If you already rank well for similar keywords, you have a better chance with a new related keyword. If your site is new, start with keywords where the top results are weak or where you can offer something genuinely different. As your site grows, harder keywords become realistic. Difficulty is relative to you, not an absolute property of the keyword.

Decide when to compete and when to wait

Do not avoid every hard keyword, and do not chase every easy one. Compete for a hard keyword when it matters a lot to your site and you can write the best page on the topic. Wait when the keyword is hard, the traffic is small, and a better use of your time is an easier keyword that brings visitors sooner. Keep a list of hard keywords to revisit later, as your site grows stronger. Review your decisions every few months, because rankings change, old pages fade, and a keyword that was hard last year may be winnable now.

Quick tips

  • Open the top ten results before trusting a score
  • Compare the competition with your own site
  • Start with weak top results
  • Revisit hard keywords as your site grows

Frequently asked questions

What is keyword difficulty?

A score from a keyword tool that estimates how hard it is to rank on the first page for a keyword, based mainly on the strength of the pages already ranking.

Why do different tools give different difficulty scores?

Each tool uses its own formula and data, so scores differ. Use the score as a guide, and always check the real top results yourself.

Should a new site avoid all difficult keywords?

Not all of them, but start with easier ones to build traffic and confidence. Save hard keywords for later, when your site is stronger.
