Question Keywords: Turn Real Questions into Traffic

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Question keywords are searches phrased as questions: how do I, what is, why does, can I, and similar phrases. They are a large share of all searches, and they are often easier to rank for because the searcher wants a clear answer, not a long sales page. A site that answers real questions well builds trust, gets visits from people who need help, and often appears in featured results. This guide explains how to find question keywords, how to answer them, and how to organise answers so they are useful to readers and to search engines.

Where question keywords come from

Question keywords come from the same places as other keywords, but questions are easiest to find in the People Also Ask box, in search suggestions, in forums, and in the questions customers ask you. Type a topic into the search box and add how, what, why, when, where or can, and watch the suggestions change. Read the questions in comments, in support emails, and in community groups in your niche. Each real question is a keyword, and each one tells you exactly what the searcher wants to know. Write down the exact wording, because people search using their own words, not yours.

Answer the question first, then explain

A good answer page starts with a direct answer in the first paragraph, then gives the details, steps and examples. Do not bury the answer at the bottom of a long introduction, because the searcher will leave. Use the question as a heading, answer it in two or three sentences, then add the explanation. This structure helps the reader and helps the search engine understand that the page answers the question. A page that answers one question well is better than a page that mentions ten questions without answering any of them properly.

Use FAQs to cover related questions

A FAQ section at the end of a page is a good place for related questions that are too small for their own page. Write each question as a heading, answer it in a short paragraph, and keep the answers direct. Do not add questions nobody asks, and do not use a FAQ to repeat the main content. A good FAQ answers the follow-up questions a reader would naturally have after reading the page. It also gives the search engine more chances to match the page to a question search.

Organise questions into a content plan

Group related questions together: a guide can answer the main question, and its FAQ can answer the follow-ups. A cluster of question pages around one topic can make your site the go-to place for that topic. Track which question pages get visits, and add new questions when readers ask them. Over time, a site that answers questions well becomes trusted, and trust brings more visits, more links and more questions to answer. Keep a list of unanswered questions from your readers, and work through it steadily.

Quick tips

  • Mine People Also Ask and forums for questions
  • Answer in the first paragraph
  • Use FAQs for follow-up questions
  • Group related questions into clusters

Frequently asked questions

What is a question keyword?

A search phrased as a question, such as how do I fix a leaking tap. It shows the searcher wants a direct answer, and it is often easier to rank for than a short keyword.

How do I find question keywords?

Use People Also Ask, search suggestions with how, what and why, forums, and the questions your readers and customers actually ask you.

Should every question get its own page?

No. A main question deserves a full page, while smaller follow-up questions fit in a FAQ section on that page.
