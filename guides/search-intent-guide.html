Search Intent: How to Tell What the Searcher Really Wants

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Search intent is the reason behind a search. Two people can type similar words and want completely different things: one wants to learn, one wants to compare, one wants to buy, and one wants to reach a specific site. A page that matches the intent will satisfy the visitor and rank better. A page that misses the intent will be ignored, even if it uses the right keyword. This guide explains the four main types of intent, how to spot them from the keyword and the results page, and how to shape your page to match. It is one of the most practical skills in keyword research.

The four types of intent

Informational intent means the searcher wants to learn something, such as how to fix a tap or what a word means. Commercial intent means they are comparing options before a decision, such as best laptop for students. Transactional intent means they are ready to act, such as buy running shoes online. Navigational intent means they want a specific site or brand, such as a login page. Most keywords lean toward one type, but some are mixed. Knowing the type tells you what kind of page to write: a guide, a comparison, a product page or a simple landing page.

Read intent from the keyword itself

The words in a keyword often give the intent away. How, what, why and guide suggest informational intent. Best, top, review, vs and comparison suggest commercial intent. Buy, price, discount, near me and order suggest transactional intent. A brand name or a site name suggests navigational intent. Look for these clues before you write, and write them down next to each keyword in your list. A keyword without a clear intent clue needs a look at the results page to decide what searchers actually want.

Read intent from the results page

Search for the keyword and look at what ranks. If the top results are guides and articles, the intent is informational. If they are comparison and review pages, it is commercial. If they are shops and product pages, it is transactional. If one brand dominates, it is navigational. The results page is the search engine telling you what it thinks searchers want, based on what they click. Match the type of page that already ranks, but make yours better. Fighting the intent by writing a different kind of page rarely works.

Match your page to the intent

An informational page should answer the question clearly, with steps, examples and a summary. A commercial page should compare options honestly, with prices, pros and cons. A transactional page should make the next step obvious, with clear prices and a simple action. A navigational page should get the visitor to the right place quickly. Do not mix intents in one page unless the keyword is genuinely mixed. A page that tries to inform, compare and sell at once usually does none of them well. Match the intent first, then write the best version of that kind of page.

Quick tips

  • Learn the four intent types
  • Look for clue words in the keyword
  • Check what type of page already ranks
  • Match the page type to the intent

Frequently asked questions

What is search intent?

The reason behind a search: to learn, to compare, to buy or to reach a specific site. Matching your page to the intent is essential for ranking and for satisfying the visitor.

How can I tell the intent of a keyword?

Look for clue words such as how, best, buy or a brand name, and check what type of pages rank for it. Guides mean informational, comparisons mean commercial, shops mean transactional.

Can a keyword have more than one intent?

Yes. Some keywords are mixed, and the results page may show different types of pages. In that case, cover the main intent first and mention the secondary one briefly.
