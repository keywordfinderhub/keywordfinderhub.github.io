How to Refresh Old Content with New Keywords

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Old content is an asset most sites ignore. A page published two years ago may still get visits, but it may also be outdated, thin, or targeting a keyword that no longer matches what people search for. Refreshing it with new keywords and fresh information is often faster and more effective than writing a new page, because the old page already has history, links and some ranking. This guide explains how to choose which pages to refresh, how to find new keywords for them, and how to update them without losing what already works.

Choose the pages worth refreshing

Start with pages that already get some traffic or rank on page two, because a small improvement can move them to page one. Also look at pages that used to get traffic and have faded, and pages that are outdated but still cover a topic people search for. Do not refresh a page that gets no traffic, ranks nowhere, and covers a topic nobody searches for; it is better to delete it or replace it. Use Search Console to find pages with impressions but few clicks, and pages that rank for queries they were not written for. Those are the best candidates for a refresh.

Find new keywords for the old page

Check what the page already ranks for in Search Console, and look for related queries it appears for but does not answer well. Use a keyword tool to find related phrases, questions and long-tail variations that have appeared since the page was written. Look at the top results for the main keyword and note what they cover that your page does not. Write down the new keywords that fit the page, and ignore the ones that would change its topic completely. A refresh should deepen the page, not turn it into a different page.

Update the page, do not just add keywords

Add the missing sections, update old facts, prices and dates, fix broken links, and improve the title and headings. Answer the new questions in the FAQ or in new sections, and use the new keywords naturally where they fit. Remove content that is no longer true or useful, and rewrite sections that are thin. Keep the page's address the same, so it keeps its history and links. After the update, check the page on a phone, because many readers will see it there first. A good refresh feels like a new page, but it keeps the old page's strength.

Track the refresh and repeat

Note the date you refreshed the page and what you changed, then check its ranking and traffic after a few weeks. A successful refresh should show more impressions, a better position, or more clicks. If it does not improve, check whether the keyword was misjudged or whether the page still misses something the top results cover. Refreshing is a routine, not a one-time job: set a schedule to review your oldest pages every few months, and refresh the ones that still matter. A site that keeps its old pages fresh will outlast a site that only publishes new ones.

Quick tips

  • Start with pages on page two
  • Find related queries in Search Console
  • Update facts, not just keywords
  • Keep the page address the same

Frequently asked questions

Should I update an old page or write a new one?

Update the old page if it already ranks or gets traffic and the topic is still relevant. A new page is better when the topic is genuinely different.

How often should I refresh old content?

Review your oldest and most important pages every few months, and refresh the ones that are outdated or close to ranking on page one.

Will changing a page hurt its ranking?

A careful refresh usually helps, because the page becomes more useful. Keep the address the same, keep the good parts, and avoid changing the topic completely.
