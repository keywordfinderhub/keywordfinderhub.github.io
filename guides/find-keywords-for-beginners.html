How to Find Keywords for Beginners: A Simple Start

A practical guide from Keyword Finder.

Keyword research sounds technical, but the idea is simple: find the words people type into a search engine when they want something you can help with. If you write about those words, in a way that genuinely answers the question, search engines can send you visitors. This guide is for complete beginners. It explains what a keyword is, how to brainstorm your first list, how to check whether a keyword is worth targeting, and how to avoid the mistakes that waste months of work. You do not need paid tools to start, and you do not need to learn everything at once.

What a keyword really is

A keyword is the phrase a person types into a search box. It can be one word, such as recipes, or a longer phrase, such as easy chicken recipes for beginners. Longer phrases are usually clearer about what the person wants, and they are often easier to rank for. A good keyword has three qualities: people actually search for it, it matches something you can write about well, and the competition is not so strong that a new site cannot compete. Start by writing down the questions your readers ask you, the problems they describe, and the words they use. Those real words are often better keywords than anything a tool suggests.

Brainstorm from your readers, not from a tool

Open a blank page and write down every topic your site covers, then write the questions people ask about each topic. Look at the comments on your posts, the messages you receive, and the questions in forums and groups in your niche. Search engines themselves give ideas: type a topic into the search box and look at the suggestions that appear, and scroll to the related searches at the bottom of the results page. Write down every idea without judging it yet. A long messy list is a good start, because you will filter it in the next step. Aim for fifty ideas before you stop.

Check whether a keyword is worth it

For each idea, ask whether people search for it, whether you can write a better page than the ones already ranking, and whether the visitor would be a good fit for your site. A keyword with no searches will bring no visitors, however good the page is. A keyword dominated by large famous sites will be hard for a new site to win. A keyword that brings visitors who leave immediately is not useful either. Use a free keyword tool to check rough search volume, and look at the top results to judge the competition. Pick a mix of easier keywords you can win soon and harder ones to aim for later.

Turn your list into a plan

Choose ten keywords to start with, and write one strong page for each one. Give each page a clear title, answer the question fully, and use the keyword naturally in the title, the first paragraph and a heading. Do not force the keyword into every sentence; that reads badly and does not help. Publish the pages, then watch which ones get visits and which ones do not. Keyword research is not a one-time job: repeat it every month, add new ideas, and improve the pages that are close to ranking. A small list, worked on steadily, beats a huge list that is never used.

Quick tips

  • Start with real questions from real readers
  • Aim for fifty ideas before filtering
  • Pick ten keywords to start with
  • Write one strong page per keyword

Frequently asked questions

What is a keyword in SEO?

A keyword is the word or phrase a person types into a search engine. In SEO it is the topic you target with a page, so that the page can appear when someone searches for it.

Do I need a paid tool to find keywords?

No. Free tools, search suggestions, related searches and questions from your readers are enough to start. Paid tools help later, when you want more data.

How many keywords should a beginner target?

Start with about ten. Write one good page for each, learn what works, then expand the list. Ten finished pages beat a hundred ideas that were never written.
